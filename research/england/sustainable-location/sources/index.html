Sustainable location: sources

Every document relied on by How to assess a sustainable location under the 2026 NPPF, with its publisher, date, what it was used for and where the checked copy is held. External sources were retrieved on 6 October 2026.

How the sources are used #

Each quotation on the summary page is checked, when the page is built, against a saved copy of the document it comes from. So are the dates given here. Documents under Crown copyright are kept in the public research repository under the Open Government Licence. Copies of other publishers' documents are kept privately and quoted only briefly.

Abbreviations: NPPF is the National Planning Policy Framework; MHCLG is the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government; DfT is the Department for Transport; PINS is the Planning Inspectorate.

The Framework #

National policy. The August 2026 Framework is the one in force.

National Planning Policy Framework, August 2026 #

National policy

Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (MHCLG). 17 August 2026.

Used for: Policy TR3 (the test for a sustainable location), GB7(1)(g)(iii), HC5, and the Annex B definitions of "connectivity" and "reasonable walking distance".

Copy held: nppf/NPPF-August-2026.txt in the research repository (Crown copyright, Open Government Licence).

National Planning Policy Framework, December 2024 #

National policy (superseded)

MHCLG. December 2024.

Used for: Paragraph 110, to show that the wording of TR3(1)(a) is carried forward from the previous Framework.

Copy held: nppf/NPPF-December-2024.txt in the research repository (Crown copyright, Open Government Licence).

The Connectivity Tool #

The tool named in TR3(2) and the Department for Transport's own guidance on using it.

Connectivity Tool #

Official tool

Department for Transport (DfT). First published 26 June 2025; last updated 3 July 2026.

Used for: What the tool is, who can use the full version, and that Connectivity Tool Lite is open to anyone.

Copy held: guidance-ogl/dft-connectivity-tool.txt in the research repository (Crown copyright, Open Government Licence).

Interpreting connectivity scores #

Official guidance

DfT. Linked from the GOV.UK page on 3 July 2026.

Used for: How scores are calculated, why 50 is not "average", the Connectivity Matrix, the worked example, and what the score does not measure.

Copy held: guidance-ogl/dft-connectivity-tool-interpreting-scores.txt in the research repository (Crown copyright, Open Government Licence).

Applying the Connectivity Tool #

Official guidance

DfT. Undated help page.

Used for: The tool's purpose in plan-making and decision-taking, that driving scores should not be the basis of decisions, and that route quality is not measured.

Copy held: guidance-ogl/dft-connectivity-tool-applying.txt in the research repository (Crown copyright, Open Government Licence).

Guidance still to come #

What the government has promised, the guidance as it stood, and a professional body's account of the position.

National Planning Policy Framework consultation: government response #

Government statement

MHCLG. August 2026.

Used for: The commitment to revise the transport planning practice guidance, and the clarification that other evidence can be used alongside the Connectivity Tool.
