10 West Street, Weston-super-Mare (shop unit merged into the dwelling above)

Site context
inside settlement, conservation area
Green Belt
No
Framework applied
August 2026 NPPF
Determinative policies
HE6(4), HE4(2), DP3(3), S4(1)
Development plan policies

Summary

A vacant shop unit in a busy secondary street next to Weston-super-Mare's Primary Shopping Area was to be absorbed into the dwelling above, with a domestic frontage in place of the shopfront. The inspector found the loss of the unit would harm town centre vitality under local policy, and that the new frontage and the loss of ground-floor commercial use would cause low-end harm to the Great Weston Conservation Area. Public benefits were limited, the harm lacked clear and convincing justification, and under S4 the benefits were substantially outweighed. Dismissed.

Issues and findings

  • Transition. "Both of the main parties to this appeal have had the opportunity to comment in light of the revised document" (DL ¶2). The Framework "makes clear that plan-making policies should not be used when making decisions on development proposals" (DL ¶2).
  • Emerging plan, DM4: limited weight. "I have not been provided with the draft policies and do not know what the final policies will be, therefore the weight to be afforded is limited" (DL ¶3).
  • Town centre (local DM60): CONFLICT. "the principal impact would be the loss of the retail premises in a location wholly appropriate for such continued use" (DL ¶10).
  • Heritage, HE5(2): HARM, low end. "The loss of the retail unit, in principle, therefore, undermines the significance of the GWCA in this location" (DL ¶18). "I find the proposal would harm the significance of the GWCA as a whole, albeit that this would be at the low end of the scale" (DL ¶19).
  • HE6(4) and HE4(2): FAIL. "I am not persuaded that the public benefits would be of sufficient magnitude to outweigh the harm to the significance of the heritage asset, and the considerable importance this carries. Therefore, the harm that would be caused to the significance of the GWCA lacks clear and convincing justification" (DL ¶24).
  • Fallback (Class MA): limited weight. "this has not been secured and there is therefore no more than a theoretical possibility that development might take place" (DL ¶28).
  • TC2 benefit: limited. "As such, the benefits to the vitality and viability of the town centre would be limited" (DL ¶33).
  • DP3(1) and DP3(3). "Policy DP3.3 requires substantial weight to be given to compliance with relevant development plan policies in relation to design quality and proposals should be refused where they lack clear justification for non-compliance in this respect" (DL ¶34).

Planning balance

HE6(4) first (DL ¶21–24), then the development plan (DL ¶31), then S4(1). "Taken together, I find the benefits of the proposal would be substantially outweighed by its adverse effects" (DL ¶35). TC2 and L2 are named as substantial-weight benefits but both are discounted on the facts: no net gain in homes, and a unit vacant only a short time. DP3(3) is paraphrased with its clear-justification test and used as a weight in the S4 balance; no separate finding on justification is made for the design conflict.

What made the difference

The street was fully let apart from this unit, which had been empty only a few months after a 10-year tenancy. That undercut both the vitality case and the L2 vacant-building benefit. With no extra dwelling, there was little on the other side of the heritage balance.

Transferable points

  • In a conservation area whose significance lies in ground-floor commercial activity, losing the retail use is itself heritage harm, apart from the design of the new frontage (DL ¶18).
  • TC2 substantial weight for residential use in a town centre is cut to limited where there is no net gain in dwellings and a retail unit is lost (DL ¶33).
  • L2 weight for reusing a vacant building depends on evidence that it is not attractive to the market; a short vacancy does not show that (DL ¶33).
  • A Class MA fallback needs prior approval to be more than "a theoretical possibility" (DL ¶28).
  • Emerging plan policies that are not put before the inspector get limited weight under DM4 (DL ¶3).
  • Low-end harm to a conservation area beat a scheme with limited public benefits (DL ¶19, ¶24).

Key facts

  • West Street adjoins the Primary Shopping Area; only the appeal unit was vacant at the site visit, with good footfall (DL 6)
  • Tattoo studio closed around December 2025 after about a 10-year tenancy; no marketing, and no policy requiring it (DL 8)
  • No net gain in dwellings - the existing upper-floor dwelling would be enlarged to 4 bedrooms (DL 10, 21)
  • Class MA permitted development fallback given limited weight - prior approval not secured, "no more than a theoretical possibility", and no means of comparing its harm (DL 27-28)
  • Flood risk - conversion from a less vulnerable to a more vulnerable use (Annex F Table 2) does not justify the proposal; neutral (DL 29)
  • Elevation changes not suitable to be left wholly to a condition in a conservation area (DL 23)
  • Site visit made on 9 July 2026; parties given the opportunity to comment on the revised Framework (DL 2)
