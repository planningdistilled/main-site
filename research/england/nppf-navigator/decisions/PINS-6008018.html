Summary

A six-bedroom guest house on the first floor of a vacant Manchester pub was dismissed. The application left out the ground floor, so the only access was through a rear yard past the bins and up a narrow stair. The inspector found the scheme was not shown to be deliverable or of the quality the local visitor-accommodation policies require, treated that as a conflict with DP3, and found the benefits substantially outweighed under S4.

Issues and findings

  • Plan weight, Annex A ¶2. "Policy CC4 of the CS and policy 4.1 of the UDP are not materially inconsistent with the national decision making policies within the Framework" (DL ¶11), "as they are policies which seek to assist in creating well designed places, which is a key principle of Framework Policy DP3" (DL ¶11).
  • Principle: CONFLICT. "I cannot be assured that the accommodation as a whole would be of an appropriate quality or offer good ease of access for all" (DL ¶16).
  • Living conditions and parking: acceptable. "There is nothing compelling to indicate that any, likely limited, increased parking demand in association with this proposal would cause any unacceptable additional parking stress or cause harm to highway safety" (DL ¶25).
  • DP3 and S4: FAIL. "There would be conflict with Policy DP3 and the benefits of approving the scheme would be substantially outweighed by the adverse effects" (DL ¶31).

Planning balance

S4(1). For: reuse of a vacant building, some employment, support for tourism (DL ¶28). Against: "serious doubts over the design quality of the scheme before me" because access for all is not assured (DL ¶29). The DP3 conflict is derived from "development plan policy relating to the design standards for this type of accommodation with regard to access" (DL ¶31). DP3(3) is not cited and no clear-justification question is asked.

What made the difference

The red line and description. By excluding the ground floor, through which the first floor must be reached, the scheme could not show a workable or good-quality access. An application covering both floors with a proper entrance would have met the main objection.

Transferable points

  • Local policies on the quality of visitor accommodation were held not materially inconsistent with the Framework, by reference to DP3, and kept full weight (DL ¶11).
  • A conflict with a local standard on access quality was treated as a DP3 conflict and weighed in the S4 balance (DL ¶31).
  • A scheme whose access depends on land or floorspace outside the application is not shown to be deliverable (DL ¶14, ¶29).

Key facts

  • The application covers the first floor only; the appellant says the ground floor has a "nil" use, yet the first floor can only be reached through it (DL 3-5)
  • CS CC4 supports hotels outside the city centre only where the Council is confident they are deliverable; deliverability not certain here (DL 13-14)
  • Bus stops and the Central Park Metrolink stop within a 10 minute walk (DL 24)
  • No harm to neighbours' living conditions from comings and goings in a largely commercial area (DL 18-21)
  • Costs application by the appellant against the Council, the subject of a separate decision (DL 2)
  • No switch paragraph; the Framework is identified in a footnote as the 2026 version (DL 11)
