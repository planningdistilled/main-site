Summary

Storage building for a joinery business on an industrial estate in Halifax, dismissed. It would take up the business's existing parking and yard, and the appellant could not show that the remaining parking, or land outside the red line, would meet the combined demand. This conflicted with TR4(1)(e) and brought in TR6(4).

What made the difference

The evidence was poor. The parking survey did not separate the site from neighbouring units, and there was no proof of control over the off-site manoeuvring areas (DL ¶¶7–9). Moderate economic benefit could not stand against "TR6(4) ... development should be refused if it would have an unacceptable impact on highway safety", to which the inspector gave significant weight. The inspector's own finding was "detrimental" rather than expressly "unacceptable" (DL ¶¶13, 18–19). Good town-centre accessibility did not help an industrial use that needs delivery access (DL ¶10).
