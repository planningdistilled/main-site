Summary

One self-build house at Clavering was dismissed although the inspector found the site to be within the village and in a sustainable location, so that S4 applied. The access crossed Flood Zones 2 and 3 with no sequential test and no demonstrated safe escape route, and the self-build unilateral undertaking (UU) was too defective to secure the biodiversity net gain (BNG) exemption. Those harms substantially outweighed the benefit of one home.

Issues and findings

  • Transition. "The parties were given an opportunity to comment on the implications of the revised Framework for the appeal" (DL ¶4).
  • Sequential test, F5: FAIL. "Part 2(b)(i) is not met as built development within the site boundary, including access or escape routes, would be located in an area that would be at risk of flooding from any source, now and in the future" (DL ¶13). "no sequential test has been undertaken, and I have not been provided with sufficient evidence to demonstrate that there are no reasonably available sites at a lower risk of flooding" (DL ¶15).
  • Safety, F7: FAIL. The flood risk assessment "does not demonstrate how the site could be safely accessed or provide definitive escape routes in a flooding event" (DL ¶12).
  • Self-build and BNG: FAIL. "On the basis of what is before me I am not satisfied that the development can be considered to be custom or self-build and the proposal has also failed to demonstrate that it would make appropriate provision for BNG" (DL ¶18).
  • Location: within the settlement, S4. "the site is perceived as forming part of the built area within the village. It does not perform the role of a distinct gap within the settlement" (DL ¶23).
  • Accessibility: PASS. "Whilst the roads are unlit, the presence of footways and the 30 miles per hour speed limit assists in making the routes reasonably accessible for pedestrians and cyclists" (DL ¶25).

Planning balance

S4(1). Against: flood risk, substantial weight (DL ¶28); failure to meet the statutory BNG provisions, significant weight (DL ¶29). For: one dwelling at 4.77 years, substantial weight (DL ¶30); biodiversity enhancements and economic benefits, moderate weight together (DL ¶31). Self-build carried no weight as it was unsecured (DL ¶19, ¶29). "the adverse effects and policy conflicts identified in relation to flood risk and BNG are considerable and weigh heavily against the proposal, such that they substantially outweigh the benefits of the proposal" (DL ¶32).

What made the difference

The access, not the house, was in the flood zone, and that alone took the scheme outside the F5(2)(b)(i) exemption. With a sequential test, evidence of a dry or safe escape route, and a properly executed self-build undertaking, the inspector's findings on location and S4 would have supported approval.

Transferable points

  • An access or escape route in Flood Zones 2 or 3 engages the sequential test under F5(2)(b)(i), even where the dwelling is in Flood Zone 1 (DL ¶13).
  • An inspector may raise the sequential test where the council did not, after inviting comments (DL ¶2, ¶8).
  • Failure of the sequential test is weighed in the S4 balance rather than treated as automatically fatal, and still attracted substantial weight (DL ¶14, ¶28).
  • A self-build UU with no title evidence, no plan and no occupancy period secures neither the BNG exemption nor any self-build benefit (DL ¶18–19).
  • Where a village has no defined boundary, whether a site is within the built area is a planning judgement on containment and the pattern of development (DL ¶21–23).

Key facts

  • Access onto Clatterbury Lane lies in Flood Zones 2 and 3 (fluvial); the dwelling would be in Flood Zone 1 (DL 7)
  • Flood level 85.800 AOD against access level 85.118 AOD, so about 682 mm depth over the access (DL 11)
  • Flood risk was not a refusal reason; the inspector raised the sequential test and invited comments (DL 2, 8)
  • UU defects: no evidence of title, no site plan in Schedule 1, no occupancy period for the commissioning household, references to plural dwellings and reserved matters (DL 18)
  • Council's supply is 4.77 years (DL 30)
  • Appellant cited Mead Realisations [2024] EWHC 279 and Gladman v SSHCLG and Lancaster [2026] EWHC 51 (Admin); the inspector weighed the absence of a sequential test in the balance, as the second case requires (DL 14)
  • Appellant commented on the August 2026 Framework; the Council did not respond (DL 4)
