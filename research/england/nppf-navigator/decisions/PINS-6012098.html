Summary

A JOLT electric vehicle charge point with advertising screens on the Old Brompton Road footway was dismissed, with its linked advertisement appeal. It would harm the setting of the Boltons Conservation Area at the lower level, and the modest public benefits did not outweigh that harm under HE6(4). Unlike the sister case at Fulham Road (PINS-6012105), it also failed on highway safety, because pedestrians naturally walked across the proposed position where the footway narrows.

Issues and findings

  • Transition. "The main parties have been consulted and provided with an opportunity to provide comments on it in relation to these appeals" (DL ¶4).
  • Heritage: harm to one conservation area, none to the other. "I do not find harm in relation to this aspect and the setting of the Thurloe/Smiths Charity CA would be preserved" (DL ¶12).
  • HE6(4): FAIL. "Whilst the degree of harm resulting from the proposed JOLT and advertisement on the significance of the Boltons CA would be at the lower level, in accordance with Policy HE6 (4) of the Framework, that harm should be weighed against any public benefits of the proposals" (DL ¶13). "I find insufficient public benefits arising from the proposals to offset the identified harm to which I attach substantial weight" (DL ¶15).
  • Highway safety: harm. "I observed many pedestrians naturally move out to the edge of the pavement and across the position of the proposed JOLT which formed a natural desire line for pedestrians in this location" (DL ¶19). The unit "would cause an undue obstruction to pedestrians and impede the free flow of pedestrian movements, including those with mobility impairments" (DL ¶20).

Planning balance

HE6(4): lower-level harm to a conservation area's setting against modest public benefits. No S4 balance is run; the conclusion is plan-led (DL ¶26).

What made the difference

Two things: the open footway within 30 m of the Boltons Conservation Area, and the pinch point next door that pushed pedestrians across the proposed position. Meeting the 2 m numerical guidance did not answer what the inspector saw on site.

Transferable points

  • Compliance with a minimum clear footway width does not settle pedestrian safety where the footway narrows nearby and the unit would sit on an observed desire line (DL ¶19–20).
  • Setting harm was found for a conservation area about 30 m away but not for one about 70 m away behind intervening buildings (DL ¶11–12).
  • Lower-level harm to a conservation area's setting outweighs the benefits of one public charge point (DL ¶13–15).

Key facts

  • Joint letter with advertisement Appeal B 6012102 (CA/26/00151), also dismissed on amenity and public safety (DL 1, 17, 22, 27)
  • Site outside a car showroom on a principal strategic route; cycle hire stations nearby (DL 7, 18)
  • Unit would leave about 2.1 m of footway and sit about 450 mm from the kerb (DL 19)
  • Benefits claimed - public on-street charging, free daily 7 kWh allowance funded by advertising, inclusive design, decarbonisation; found modest and achievable in a less harmful manner (DL 14-15, 25)
  • Same inspector dismissed a similar unit outside 131 Fulham Road (PINS-6012105) on the same day
  • Parties consulted on the August 2026 Framework (DL 4)
