Summary

Remodelling of a heavily extended 1960s bungalow into a chalet-style house with a new roof storey and six dormers. Dismissed. At roughly 170-270% of the original it was disproportionate under GB7(1)(b), with limited openness harm, poor design and an inadequate FRA. No VSC.

Issues and findings

  • Transition. The changes "do not affect the merits of this case in a material way", so the parties were not consulted (DL ¶2).
  • GB7(1)(b): FAIL. "around 170% of the original 1960s bungalow … around 270% if a first floor is included … Both figures indicate a disproportionate addition" (DL ¶9).
  • Openness: "localised and limited … Whilst harm to the Green Belt would be limited in nature … it nevertheless attracts substantial weight" (DL ¶13).
  • Design and flood risk: conflict (DL ¶17-18, 24).

Planning balance

GB6(2) is "a high hurdle"; the limited considerations do not clearly outweigh (DL ¶27, 30).

What made the difference

Cumulative enlargement well beyond the original building, compounded by poor design.

Transferable points

  • Limited, localised openness harm still attracts substantial weight under GB6(2) (DL ¶13, 27).
