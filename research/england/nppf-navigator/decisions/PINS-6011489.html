Lot B2a, Upper Bourne End Lane, Hemel Hempstead (outdoor wellbeing use and office unit)

Procedure
written representations
Development

Site context
green belt, open countryside
Green Belt
Yes (grey belt not argued)
Framework applied
August 2026 NPPF
Determinative policies
GB7(1)(f)(iv), GB6(2)

Main issues
inappropriate development; highway safety; very special circumstances

Summary

A small plot of Green Belt pasture near Hemel Hempstead was to be used for appointment-only outdoor wellbeing sessions, with a prefabricated office unit for consultations. The inspector accepted the use of the land as outdoor recreation under GB7(1)(f)(iii) but found the building was not an "appropriate facility" under (f)(iv): nothing showed it was needed for the outdoor use. The scheme was inappropriate, with added harm to highway safety and character, and very special circumstances were not shown. Dismissed.

Issues and findings

  • Transition. "The main parties were given the opportunity to comment on this" (DL ¶1).
  • GB7(1)(f)(iii) use of land: PASS. "I accept that use of the land for the purposes stated would be recreational, and this would be consistent with part iii of Policy GB7.1.f of the Framework" (DL ¶6).
  • GB7(1)(f)(iv) building: FAIL. "I cannot see any reason why the provision of the proposed building is necessary to support those uses" (DL ¶6). "such activities do not depend on a Green Belt location and there is no robust evidence to explain how such consultations are connected to an outdoor recreation or sports use" (DL ¶6). "I consider the proposed building would not represent an appropriate facility for the use of the land for outdoor recreation" (DL ¶8).
  • Inappropriate. "the development the subject of this appeal does not fall within a category set out in Policy GB7 of the Framework" (DL ¶9).
  • Highway safety: HARM (local plan), significant weight. "With there being no robust evidence that adequate parking can be provided, I consider the proposals would be harmful to highway safety" (DL ¶11).
  • Character: HARM, moderate weight (DL ¶12).

Planning balance

GB6(2) very special circumstances. Substantial weight to Green Belt harm, significant weight to highway safety harm and moderate weight to character harm, against little or very little weight to the therapeutic benefit, the operating controls and reversibility. "I do not consider the matters raised in support of the proposal to clearly outweigh the harm identified" (DL ¶18).

What made the difference

The building, not the use. The outdoor activity was accepted as recreation. The office unit failed because consultations could happen anywhere and it filled much of a very small plot. Evidence tying a smaller shelter to the safe running of the outdoor sessions might have met (f)(iv).

Transferable points

  • The appropriate-facilities limb, GB7(1)(f)(iv), is read as a need test: the building must be shown to be necessary to support the outdoor use (DL ¶6, ¶8).
  • An activity that does not depend on a Green Belt location (one-to-one consultations) does not make its building a facility for outdoor recreation (DL ¶6).
  • A building that takes up a large part of a small site undermines the claim that the land is used for outdoor recreation (DL ¶7).
  • Reversibility and a temporary permission do not make a building appropriate or remove its effect on openness (DL ¶14).

Key facts

  • Very small plot among buildings and small plots reached by a long shared access track (DL 4, 6)
  • Proposal offered no client parking and one operator or volunteer vehicle; the inspector could not see how a condition would address parking that is neither proposed nor existing (DL 10)
  • Controls on hours and visitor numbers, reversibility and a temporary permission each given very little weight (DL 13-14)
  • Therapeutic benefit to clients given little weight (DL 15)
  • Site visit made on 17 August 2026; parties given the opportunity to comment on the revised Framework (DL 1)
