Summary

Permission in principle (PIP) for one dwelling in the Green Belt on the edge of Hitchin was allowed. The inspector found the site to be grey belt, although it adjoins a large built-up area and sits in a parcel the Council's review rates as strong, because containment made its own contribution moderate. GB7(1)(g) was met and the S5(5) balance favoured approval.

Issues and findings

  • Transition. "The main parties were given an opportunity to comment on the implications of the revised Framework for the appeal" (DL ¶5).
  • Purpose (a): moderate. Because of the site's partial enclosure, "the proposed development would not result in an incongruous pattern of development. These features would also serve to restrict and contain the proposed development to an extent" (DL ¶12).
  • Purpose (b): moderate. "Due to its small size and the existing intervening features between the towns, including roads and vegetation, the site could be developed without resulting in the loss of visual separation between the towns" (DL ¶13).
  • Grey belt: PASS. "the site does not strongly contribute to the relevant purposes of the Green Belt and therefore comprises grey belt land" (DL ¶15).
  • GB7(1)(g)(iii) / TR3: PASS. "There are bus stops and footpaths in close proximity to the site which would be available for use by future occupants to access Hitchin town centre. Due to its proximity to, and access to, different modes of transport, the site would be in a sustainable location" (DL ¶19). On the missing frontage footway: "Even if it was considered as part of this first stage, due to the scale of the development, it would not result in a significant adverse impact on the transport network" (DL ¶20).
  • S5(5), not S5(1)(j). "the decision is to be made by applying the approach set out in part (5) of Policy S5 rather than part (1)(j)" (DL ¶28).
  • Openness. "there is no need to consider its impact on the openness of the Green Belt" (DL ¶22).

Planning balance

S5(5). Substantial weight to one dwelling under HO7 and limited weight to economic benefits (DL ¶30). Limited weight to the conflict with Local Plan SP2 (DL ¶29). "I have not identified any adverse effects that would substantially outweigh the benefits" (DL ¶31).

What made the difference

Containment: housing to the west, an elevated railway to the south and vegetation to the east meant the site itself made only a moderate contribution to purpose (a), whatever the rating of the wider parcel. The location limb was passed on a general statement of nearby bus stops and footpaths, with the missing frontage footway deferred to the technical details stage. Evidence that the walk involved the carriageway might have produced a different answer on limb (iii).

Transferable points

  • Land adjoining a town can still be grey belt: containment features of the kind listed in Annex E reduce the contribution to purpose (a) to moderate (DL ¶11–12).
  • The site is assessed, not the review parcel: a parcel rated strong for purposes (a) and (b) did not prevent a moderate finding for a small enclosed plot (DL ¶10–13).
  • Where a council argues S5(1)(j) on a Green Belt site, S5(5) is the correct route (DL ¶28).
  • Once GB7(1)(g) is met, openness is not assessed (DL ¶22).
  • Caution: limb (iii) was passed without distances, frequencies or a route assessment (DL ¶19–20), so the letter is weak authority on the sustainable-location test.

Key facts

  • PIP stage 1, so only location, land use and amount are considered (DL 3)
  • North Herts Green Belt Review 2016 rates the wider parcel as a strong contribution to purposes (a) and (b) (DL 10, 13)
  • Council's response on the new Framework raised the lack of a footway at the site frontage; not a refusal reason (DL 20)
  • Site about 500 m from Great Wymondley; no appreciable harm to heritage settings there (DL 27)
  • Parties consulted on the August 2026 Framework and both responded (DL 5)
