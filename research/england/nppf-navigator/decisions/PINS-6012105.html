Summary

A JOLT electric vehicle charge point with double-sided advertising screens on the Fulham Road footway was dismissed, with its linked advertisement appeal. The unit would be the most prominent feature in an open, uncluttered footway and would harm the setting of the Thurloe/Smiths Charity Conservation Area at the lower level. The public benefits of on-street charging were modest and achievable less harmfully, so they did not outweigh the harm under HE6(4). Pedestrian safety was acceptable.

Issues and findings

  • Transition. "The main parties have been consulted and provided with an opportunity to provide comments on it in relation to these appeals" (DL ¶4).
  • Character: harm. "it would have an undue presence in its setting, appearing as an incongruous and uncharacteristic feature" (DL ¶9).
  • HE6(4): FAIL. "Whilst the degree of harm resulting from the proposed JOLT and advertisement on the significance of the CA would be at the lower level, in accordance with Policy HE6 (4) of the Framework, that harm should be weighed against any public benefits of the proposals" (DL ¶12). "I find insufficient public benefits arising from the proposals to offset the identified harm to which I attach substantial weight" (DL ¶14).
  • Less harmful alternatives. The benefits would be modest and "there is no indication that they could not be achieved in a less harmful manner than proposed" (DL ¶25).
  • Highway safety: acceptable. The unit "would not have any additional significant impact in this respect and would, in my view, leave ample room for the free and safe movement of pedestrians" (DL ¶19).

Planning balance

HE6(4): lower-level harm to a conservation area's setting against modest public benefits. No S4 balance is run; the conclusion is that the scheme is "not in accordance with the development plan when read as a whole" (DL ¶26).

What made the difference

The open, lightly furnished footway opposite the conservation area. A unit replacing existing street furniture, as with the BT Street Hub the appellant cited, would have been judged against a different baseline.

Transferable points

  • Lower-level harm to a conservation area's setting from a single item of street furniture outweighs the benefits of one public charge point (DL ¶12–14).
  • Benefits that could be achieved "in a less harmful manner" are discounted in the HE6(4) balance (DL ¶25).
  • A footway width just below a council's SPD standard is not harmful where the unit does not interrupt a pedestrian desire line (DL ¶18–19).

Key facts

  • Joint letter with advertisement Appeal B 6012107 (CA/26/00163), also dismissed on amenity; public safety acceptable (DL 1, 16, 22, 27)
  • Site in the Brompton Cross district centre; the conservation area boundary is on the opposite side of Fulham Road, so the harm is through the setting (DL 7, 11)
  • Benefits claimed - public on-street charging, free daily 7 kWh allowance funded by advertising, inclusive design, decarbonisation; found modest given the limited scale and achievable in a less harmful manner (DL 13-14, 25)
  • BT Street Hub approved at 97 Fulham Road distinguished, as it replaced an existing kiosk (DL 23)
  • The highway section refers to "King's Road" although the site is on Fulham Road (DL 17)
  • Same inspector dismissed a similar unit at 125-133 Old Brompton Road (PINS-6012098) on the same day
  • Parties consulted on the August 2026 Framework (DL 4)
