Summary

Retention for three years of a modern pergola in front of a Grade II listed villa used as a private hospital in Mill Hill. The inspector found that it competes with the balanced principal elevation and harms the building's significance through its setting. The benefits were private to users of the facility. Dismissed, as was the sister appeal for the rear pergola (PINS-6011855).

Issues and findings

  • Transition. "Whilst the format has altered, any considerations that are material to this decision have not fundamentally changed" (DL ¶5).
  • Harm: HE6(3). "it disrupts the balanced composition and refined late Georgian proportions that underpin the building's special interest" (DL ¶10). "I therefore find that the proposal would result in harm to the significance of the designated heritage asset, to which I attach considerable importance and weight" (DL ¶11).
  • Public benefits: HE6(4) FAIL. "such advantages are inherently private in nature and do not amount to public benefits in the heritage context" (DL ¶12).
  • Conflict. "The development therefore conflicts with Framework Policy HE6" (DL ¶14).

Planning balance

HE6(4): harm against public benefits, run before the development plan conclusion. The letter attributes the weighing to "Policy HE5" (DL ¶11); the weighing is in HE6(4), and the conclusion at DL ¶14 cites HE6. The slip is incidental.

What made the difference

A conspicuous modern structure on the principal elevation of a listed villa, with no benefit to anyone but the facility's users.

Transferable points

  • Size and prominence that "compete with, rather than defer to, the host building" harm significance even with no effect on fabric (DL ¶10–11).
  • Private benefits to users of a private healthcare facility do not count as public benefits under HE6(4) (DL ¶12).

Key facts

  • Early 19th century suburban villa listed in 1983, in private healthcare use for about 20 years; largely unaltered symmetrical principal elevation (DL 7-8)
  • Pergola stands in front of the right-hand wing by the reception; it does not touch the listed fabric (DL 9, 11)
  • Three-year temporary period gave only limited weight because the harm would persist throughout (DL 13)
  • Sister appeal 6011855 (rear pergola) dismissed by the same inspector on the same day in a separate letter (DL 4)
  • Parties not consulted on the August 2026 Framework (DL 5)
